# First 50 Learners: Pilot Outreach Playbook

You do not need to personally know 50 students. Start by asking a few people who already gather learners to share one small, useful activity.

## Goal

Find 50 learners who can complete the five-minute Student Mission, then learn whether they use the resulting Study Sprint in a real study session.

This is a learning pilot, not a marketing stunt. Do not promise better grades, collect unnecessary personal data, or pressure anyone to participate.

## Start with groups of 5–10

Ask for a small introduction, not a large commitment.

| Who to ask | Where to find them | Simple offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teachers and tutors | Local schools, tutoring centers, educator communities | A free 15-minute AI literacy activity for a class or study group. |
| Student clubs | Universities, colleges, hackathons, coding clubs | A fun AI trust challenge followed by a practical study sprint. |
| Librarians | Public libraries, school libraries, community centers | A beginner-friendly AI session with no account required. |
| Youth and nonprofit organizers | STEM groups, youth centers, community organizations | A short workshop about using AI without outsourcing thinking. |
| Friends-of-friends | Alumni groups, parent networks, neighborhood groups | Five volunteers to test a study mission and give honest feedback. |

## A realistic path to 50 learners

1. **Week 1 — five learners:** Ask two trusted contacts for five volunteers. Watch or discuss their experience closely.
2. **Week 2 — twenty learners:** Run the revised mission with two groups of ten, such as a class, club, library event, or study group.
3. **Week 3 — fifty learners:** Ask the groups that found it useful to share it with one more group.

The first goal is not reach. It is finding the language and mission outcome people care about enough to share.

## Copy-and-send invitation

> Hi! I am testing a free five-minute AI learning mission for students. It helps learners use AI for studying without copying answers or trusting unsupported claims. It ends with a personalized 20-minute Study Sprint they can use immediately. Would you be open to sharing it with 5–10 students or trying it in a short session? I am looking for honest feedback, not promotion. No account is required.

## Fun pilot formats

### 1. The AI Trust Relay

- Put learners in pairs.
- Each pair completes the Truth Relay and argues for the safest next action.
- Reveal the answer and ask: “What would you verify before acting on this?”
- Finish with the Student Mission.

### 2. Bring Your Worst Prompt

- Learners bring one vague prompt they have actually used.
- They improve it using the prompt lesson and compare the old and new result.
- They keep the improved version in their Study Sprint.

### 3. Agent Escape Room

- Give groups a fictional AI agent with access to a calendar, files, messages, and spending.
- Each group has five minutes to decide what to allow, block, and require approval for.
- Use the Safety route to discuss why permission boundaries matter.

### 4. Human vs. Model Court

- Show a persuasive AI answer with one unsupported claim.
- Learners act as judges: accept, challenge, or request evidence.
- The group must explain the evidence standard before seeing the answer.

## Questions to ask after the mission

Use a short form, a conversation, or a GitHub issue. Ask only what you need.

1. What did you expect this mission to be about?
2. Which step was most useful?
3. Which step was confusing, boring, or too long?
4. Did you copy or use the Study Sprint afterward?
5. Would you send this to a friend? Why or why not?
6. What mission should exist next?

## What to measure

Keep it simple and anonymized where possible.

- Number of people who start and finish a mission.
- Number who copy a final artifact.
- Number who say they used the artifact later that day or week.
- One-sentence feedback about the most confusing moment.
- Number of people who would recommend it.

Do **not** claim that the product measures intelligence, cognitive load, grades, or mental health.

## Turn learners into contributors

Make contribution a ladder, not a demand:

1. **Learner:** completes a mission.
2. **Reviewer:** reports one confusing phrase or checks one source.
3. **Sharer:** hosts a small AI Trust Relay with friends or classmates.
4. **Contributor:** improves copy, tests accessibility, adds a glossary term, or proposes a mission.
5. **Champion:** helps run a classroom, club, library, or community pilot.

Thank contributors publicly when appropriate, but always ask before naming or tagging anyone.

## When to expand

Do not chase a larger launch until a small group shows three signals:

- learners finish the mission;
- learners use the artifact in a real task;
- learners invite or recommend it to someone else.

That is the beginning of organic reach.
